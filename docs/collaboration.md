## Purpose

This document describes how we appreciate collaboration in the `trustoverip/dtgwg-general` repository. 

The goal is to maintain a space where **human contributors** exchange ideas, 
challenge each other's thinking, and build consensus around the Decentralized Trust Graph 
and related topics. The information below describe practices that help us do that.


---
## AI-Assisted Contributions

**We welcome** the use of AI-assisted writing tools as drafting aids. 
If, for example, an AI agent helps you organize your thoughts, tighten your phrasing, 
or catch a gap in your reasoning, that is perfectly fine. 

**Important** is that the final submission is yours: shaped by your understanding, 
based on your writing style, reflecting your position, or built on facts you have verified.

**Submitting AI-generated text** without reviewing, shaping, or taking ownership 
of it does not support the kind of collaboration we want to encourage. 
Unedited AI-generated text often ends up much longer than necessary or sound 
awkwardly poetic. This can be exhausting to read, which may discourage others 
from contributing, and make discussions less engaging for everyone.


#### Accountability
Another core reason for this principle is *accountability*. 
Every contribution should have a human who is willing to take ownership of it.
If you did not write or shape it, it should not have your name. Before submitting, 
it is worth asking yourself:

- Does this sound like me?
- Could I defend this? 

The answer should be *yes* in both cases.

## Names and GitHub Handles

When referring to fellow community members or contributor, please use their GitHub handles 
(`@username`).

Contributors sometimes prefer to separate their professional (or other) identity from their 
open-source activity. Using their handle helps respect how they have chosen to participate 
in this community. If you happen to know someone's "real" name, please don't assume 
they are comfortable with it being used here unless they have said that they are.


#### Practical Dimension

There is a practical side to it too. GitHub notifications and search 
rely on `@mentions`, so using handles ensures that people actually get notified
when they are referenced. Handle-first interaction also helps prevent implied 
hierarchies based on titles, affiliations, or external reputation.
